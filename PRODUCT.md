# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Developers, CS students, and high school teachers who find their way here through a GitHub link, a blog post, or a project README. They're technically literate but span a wide range — a 16-year-old using CTFKit in class and a backend dev reading a Go post are both valid visitors. They're not here to be impressed; they're here to find something useful or interesting.

## Product Purpose

nerdtx.net is jkw's personal technical home. It hosts open-source projects (CTFKit, CS textbooks), a technical blog, and miscellaneous tools. Success looks like: a visitor finds a project, uses it, or reads a post and comes back. The site earns trust through craft and directness, not through marketing language.

## Brand Personality

Playful / Nerdy / Warm

The nerd in nerdtx has wit. This isn't a sterile technical showcase or a corporate portfolio — it's a personal site with a voice. Texas-rooted identity (`>_` logo, flag motif) without cowboy-kitsch. Terminal-native aesthetic (monospace, CLI idioms) that stays warm and inviting rather than intimidating. Think: a smart friend who's genuinely excited about what they built and wants to show you.

## Anti-references

- **Silicon Valley SaaS**: clean white backgrounds, blue CTAs, feature grids, hero metrics, "trusted by X teams"
- **GitHub-clone dev portfolio**: green commit graphs, avatar+bio header, project card grid with star counts, README-as-homepage
- **Hacker News / utilitarian**: raw HTML aesthetic, zero visual craft, no color or type hierarchy, link-list everything
- **Over-designed agency portfolio**: scroll-jacking, WebGL backgrounds, cursor effects, 10-second load times, motion for motion's sake

## Design Principles

1. **Nerd with personality.** Technical credibility and warmth aren't opposites. The site should feel like it was made by someone who cares about craft in the same way they care about code — with precision and a bit of joy.
2. **Texas, not Texas.** The Texan identity lives in the `>_` logo and the flag motif, not in boots and longhorns. (Aggies, not Longhorns.)
3. **Terminal-native, not terminal-trapped.** Monospace font and CLI idioms are core to the identity. They serve warmth and legibility — not darkness and intimidation.
4. **Show the work, not the performer.** Projects and posts are the stars. The site is the frame. No hero self-promotion copy, no "I'm a passionate developer who loves solving problems."
5. **Handmade, not generated.** The site should feel like it was made with intention — specific choices, slight idiosyncrasy, not the output of a template.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Roboto Mono is used throughout — readable at all weights. Reduced-motion support required for any animations. Color palette passes contrast at body text sizes: navy `#233551` on cream `#f3ecdf` checks at approximately 7:1.
